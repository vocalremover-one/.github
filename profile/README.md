# VocalRemover.one

**VocalRemover.one is an online AI audio separation website for isolating vocals, extracting instrumental backing tracks, and removing drums from songs.** Work with an existing recording in your browser, listen to the separated result, and choose the audio you need for rehearsal, study, or a creative project.

**[Visit the website](https://vocalremover.one/) · [Remove drums](https://vocalremover.one/drum-remover) · [Read our tutorials](https://github.com/vocalremover-one/vocal-separation-guides)**

## Vocal separation

Separate the singing voice from the rest of a recording. The vocal track helps you follow words, phrasing, breaths, and the end of a sung line without the full arrangement competing for attention.

Use the [Acapella Extractor](https://vocalremover.one/acapella-extractor) when the voice is the part you want to keep. It can support vocal study, lyric transcription, and preparation of vocal material for editing. Compare difficult passages with the original: backing voices, reverb, or instrument bleed may still be present, and a separated vocal is not the same as an original studio vocal recording.

## Instrumental and backing track extraction

Reduce the vocal while keeping the musical backing. The [Instrumental Maker](https://vocalremover.one/instrumental-maker) is a starting point for singing practice, cover rehearsals, and karaoke-style backing tracks.

A useful rehearsal track lets you hear your own entrance and phrasing while following the accompaniment. Listen for residual lead vocals and for missing instrumental details at the same timestamp in the original. A quiet vocal echo may matter less than losing the piano or guitar cue you use to enter the song.

## Drum removal for practice

Use the [Drum Remover](https://vocalremover.one/drum-remover) to play along with a song while its original drum part is muted. The workflow separates the recording into stems and starts playback without the drums. Bring the drums back when you want to compare your part with the source.

After unlocking the result, you can export a drumless WAV mix or download the individual stems. This is useful for practicing a groove, rehearsing transitions, or preparing a backing track for a drum cover.

Listen to a busy section before choosing a result. Cymbals or kick drums can leave traces in other stems, and some percussion may disappear along with the drums. Check that the remaining bass and instruments still give you enough rhythmic reference for the exercise.

## Four stem separation

The [Stem Splitter](https://vocalremover.one/stem-splitter) separates vocals, drums, bass, and other instruments. Choose this workflow when you want to study individual parts or work with separate tracks rather than only a vocal and an instrumental.

For example, compare the bass and drum stems at the same timestamp to study how the parts fit together. Always return to the original when a detail in an isolated stem sounds uncertain.

## Hear an example first

[VocalRemover.one](https://vocalremover.one/) separates a mixed recording into a vocal track and an instrumental. To hear what that sounds like before uploading anything, compare the **original**, **vocals**, and **instrumental** in the demo on the homepage. Follow the end of the same sung phrase in all three versions. You can hear which track keeps the singer’s reverb after the voice stops.

## Which track do you need?

| What you’re working on | Where to start | What to listen for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A backing track for rehearsal | [Instrumental Maker](https://vocalremover.one/instrumental-maker) | Sing over the preview at rehearsal volume. A faint harmony may blend with your voice, while a clearly audible lead can make it hard to practice your own phrasing. |
| A vocal for transcription or a remix | [Acapella Extractor](https://vocalremover.one/acapella-extractor) | For transcription, check that you can hear consonants and where each phrase ends. For a remix, listen in the gaps between lines for cymbals or chords that could clash with the new beat. |
| A backing track for drum practice | [Drum Remover](https://vocalremover.one/drum-remover) | Play a short groove over the drumless preview. Check that the remaining instruments provide clear timing cues and that percussion residue does not mask your own hits. |
| A drum part or bass line to learn by ear | [Stem Splitter](https://vocalremover.one/stem-splitter) | Choose the four-track split for vocals, drums, bass, and other instruments. Compare the bass and drum tracks at the same timestamp to hear how the bass notes line up with the kick. |

## Getting a representative preview

Upload an MP3, WAV, M4A, or FLAC file. If you have several copies of the recording, start with the original WAV or FLAC, or the highest-quality MP3 you have. Converting a low-bitrate MP3 to WAV preserves the compression artifacts already in the recording.

The free preview covers **up to the first 60 seconds** and is available for playback. If the song opens with a long instrumental intro, that preview tells you little about vocal removal. To test a difficult chorus before buying, make a short excerpt from your source file with the chorus at the start and upload it for a preview. Use the complete original when you’re ready to process the whole song.

## Downloads

For a song longer than the preview, choose full-song processing from the result page. You can review the paid options there. Once the complete track is ready, choose the stem you need. MP3 is convenient for a rehearsal playlist. WAV avoids another lossy encoding step when you bring the stem into an audio editor.

For help with a result, email [support@vocalremover.one](mailto:support@vocalremover.one). Include the tool you used, the stem you were listening to, and a timestamp where the issue is easiest to hear.

## Tutorials and listening exercises

Our public [Vocal Separation Guides](https://github.com/vocalremover-one/vocal-separation-guides) repository contains practical listening exercises for working with isolated vocals and backing tracks.

Start with [How to judge a vocal separation by ear](https://github.com/vocalremover-one/vocal-separation-guides/blob/main/guides/judge-vocal-separation.md). It explains how to compare identical passages in the original recording, vocal stem, and instrumental, and how to decide whether a result suits rehearsal, transcription, or a remix.

The listening method is useful for drum removal too: choose a specific passage, identify the sound you need to keep or remove, and judge the result in the context of your practice session.
